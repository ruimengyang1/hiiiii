# Foundry / Future States — final report

## 1. Current game audit

The actual launch build was **System Link**. The worker baited a direction-locked
ram, rebounded across an entry ledge, moved a five-stop engineer cart, activated
an elevated safety cut-off, hit a ram-only lock, and delivered the passenger.
Cart stops set circuit flags and two scripted conditions refused unsafe moves.
Stomps directly transferred player momentum into the Can. There was no physical
plate, force-fractured material, or shared crusher in that launch route.

The preserved `game.tscn` contains two older dexterity levels: movement/rebound
over gaps and crushers, then aimed enemy dash chains with saved level unlocks.
Patrols, drones, fixed rebound devices, spikes and moving platforms mostly act
as isolated traversal obstacles there. No projectile system or separate NPC
scene was present. Character art, animation, particles and WAV audio are
generated by the existing scripts rather than imported asset pipelines.

Reused: the controller, coyote time/buffering/variable jump, health and damage,
ram renderer and signals, cart body/passenger art and support, crusher body and
individual spike polygons, switch renderer, fonts, camera, particles and sound.
The complete ten-point audit is in `DESIGN_SPEC.md`.

## 2. Final core rules

1. Can searches; nearby ground-level player position determines a visible
   0.78-second lock, followed by a straight 1.65-second charge. No homing.
2. Sufficient charge momentum uses a shared force contract: move transport or
   permanently break a fractured bulkhead. Solids interrupt the routine.
3. Stomp gives a surface-anchored rebound and grounds/stuns Can for six seconds.
   Press spikes also stun it. The grounded shell is safe, heavy and unavailable
   as force until recovery; active magnetic suspension does not load a plate.
4. Cart is engineer transport, a rebound roof/platform, and persistent heavy
   weight. Rail movement is reversible; blocked movement stalls and resumes.
5. Physical weight powers gates; either Cart or grounded Can qualifies. Levers
   latch circuits. Occupancy prevents a gate closing through a body. Small
   levers supply no rebound energy.

The player retains move, jump and stomp throughout. Stronger air steering and a
wider stomp query reduce execution cost without increasing speed or abilities.

## 3. Final core loop

Read Can's direction and state, inspect roof/weight/route positions, decide
which useful state to spend or preserve, position the worker to produce an
interaction, then evaluate the resulting configuration. The next plan starts
from the actual result. Every micro-interaction does not reset the world.

## 4. Strategic depth

Depth exists in the basic actor/cart/plate system. Stomp buys lift and safety but
spends six seconds of available force. Moving the Cart removes its former roof
and weight. Grounding Can substitutes for that weight but ties up the force
source. A permanent release costs a trip across the obstacle but frees both
objects afterward. There are no currency or ability upgrades.

The first high ventilation route needs the roof at A. Blind A→B progress removes
that access; reversing the same Cart restores it. Moving B→C removes weight and
can stall the Cart at the gate. A stopped Cart creates a new useful platform,
so a mistake changes the plan rather than ending the run. In the final yard,
isolating the press improves safety while removing its automatic Can-stun
opportunity. A manual stomp can replace that opportunity at a cost.

Normal jumping, ground-Can rebound and arbitrary repeated lever strikes cannot
replace the high roof. Progress needs cargo delivery as well as worker access,
so a strong platformer cannot finish by jumping past the object system.

## 5. Positional economy

| Resource | Useful now | Cost of consuming/changing it |
|---|---|---|
| Active Can on useful side | available force | charge relocates it; collision requires recovery |
| Stunned Can | safe lift and plate weight | six seconds without force; weight expires |
| Cart at roof dock | high route | advancing removes that roof |
| Cart on plate | lasting gate power | transport away removes power |
| Player on an upper perch | observation; lowered-fork search | another position is needed to provoke charge |
| Latched release | reliable future route | player must first reach its other side |
| Fractured passage | lasting shorter ground route | requires preparing an impact |
| Press enabled | automatic Can stun | unsafe worker traversal |
| Press isolated | safe traversal | automatic weight preparation is unavailable |

## 6. Learning curve

These labels are design notes, not in-game heuristic lessons.

| Stage | Likely novice interpretation | Learning/refinement |
|---|---|---|
| React | Can is an enemy to avoid | H0→H1: its announced direction commits; stomp is lift but spends readiness |
| Discover | Cart is stranded scenery/cargo | H2: an enemy attack can move the world |
| Generalize | Can specifically pushes this Cart | H3: the same force breaks another property-compatible object |
| Reverse | Press is only a player hazard | H4: machinery also changes Can, creating a safe bounce window |
| Choose | Any forward transport is useful | H5/H6: Can readiness and Cart roof position are resources |
| Refine | More forward distance means more progress | H7/H8: roof and weight access can be lost; reverse or preserve first |
| Return | The early sealed pump was inaccessible scenery | H9: unchanged abilities plus new understanding open an old route |
| Mastery | Handle each obstacle as it arrives | H10: prepare roof, force, weight and release in a deliberate order |
| Payoff | Charge was something to fear | The same charge is now a deliberately placed evacuation tool |

## 8. Intended first-time walkthrough

1. Meet Can in inspection. Watch the long lock, dodge a straight charge, stomp
   its top and use the bounce to reach TRACK RELEASE. The sealed service wall
   behind spawn remains an understandable visible landmark.
2. Enter the transfer line and find the stranded engineer. Passing the Cart
   naturally places the worker ahead of the following Can. Its locked impact
   moves cargo. Reproduce that impact once to enter the freight line.
3. Cargo stops at the raised track pin. From the upper service perch, Can lowers
   its forks and searches under transport. With Cart left behind, redirect an
   actual charge into the fractured bulkhead and keep the opened passage.
4. Reach the press. Its marked spike region can stun Can. During the generous
   raised phase, use the grounded shell to reach FREIGHT RELEASE. This opens
   both freight barriers; any stalled transport continues from its actual spot.
5. Cart reaches ROOF dock. Take stock: roof access opens ventilation; a charge
   advances cargo to weight power. Use the roof before consuming its position,
   or observe the loss and deliberately reverse Cart to recover it.
6. The next move vacates the plate. Preserve weight while crossing to HOLD OPEN,
   or replace Cart with a grounded Can. A poorly ordered push stalls visibly;
   reloading the plate restores movement without a password or reset.
7. The forward service bay leads back along the working facility. Cargo may wait
   at the evacuation power barrier while the worker brings the same Can to the
   early service wall. Apply known force, strike EVAC PUMP, keep all prior work.
8. In delivery, choose a plan. The Cart's high isolation roof can make the press
   safe. A durable release frees Can for force; a grounded Can instead supports
   cargo while its wheels leave the plate. Later switches only latch circuits
   already seen. A stalled roof can create recovery access to the upper route.
9. Engineer arrives. No new strategic room follows. Use the exit service perch
   to let Can search below the delivered Cart, bait it into the final fractured
   door, dodge/bounce through and reach the evacuation terminal.

R restores the current encounter; death does so after 0.4 seconds. Shift+R
starts a fresh run. Checkpoints preserve prior work, not an inferred stop index.

## 9. Experienced-player walkthrough

The expert reads direction before moving, deliberately spends stun to preserve
the roof, uses upper perches to reposition a searching Can, takes high circuits
before moving transport, and anticipates plate release. A durable route costs
less temporary commitment; a weight route spends an extra stomp and leaves Can
temporarily behind. The worker understands that it must eventually join the
escape rather than abandoning it with the delivered Cart.

The automated fresh routes measure about **3:12** (durable release/stalled-roof
recovery) and **3:18** (Can replacement weight). Both use seven transport impacts
and finish with zero deaths or rewinds. The latter spends an additional stomp.
They are input-only integration playtests, not measurements of human learning.
An expert may activate the pump early; a rule/geometry fixture validates that
the world accepts this ordering without a stage restriction.

## 10. Alternative strategies and test evidence

Two complete fresh runs validated different mastery orders:

- **Durable routing:** use the Cart's relocated/stalled roof to reach the upper
  service route, latch the far release, let transport proceed, retain Can force.
- **Temporary routing:** commit a stomp-stunned Can as plate weight after cargo
  moves away, cross while it holds power, latch the release afterward and recover
  Can for escape.

The greybox also validates physical crusher stun as the same eligible shell
state, reverse impacts recovering a lost roof, and plate restoration resuming
stalled transport. The complete fresh automatic-crusher-weight route has not
been separately scripted. This is distinct from the tested manual-stomp route.

| Required test | Evidence |

| A — Always push right | physical weight loss stalls cargo; high roof removal requires recovery |
| B — One heuristic | lift, force, weight and permanent release have different state costs |
| C — Dexterity bypass | real normal-jump/Can-bounce attempts fail the high circuit; Cart roof succeeds; small levers do not launch |
| D — Transfer | shared sufficient-force response breaks material without checking a Can ID; fresh route performs the Can impact |
| E — Consequence | roof relocation and pressure release alter access and movement |
| F — Knowledge difference | both ordered fresh routes finish without resets; roof preservation and lost-weight fixtures expose unnecessary work |

## 12. Remaining problems and final design check

- **Human timing is unmeasured.** 10–15 minutes is the intended first-time target;
  automated expert routes establish executability and a faster mastery path.
  Independent novice sessions should verify both timing and inferred heuristics.
- **The return is intentionally long.** Outrunning Can's notice range makes it
  search slowly; keeping its routine engaged can save time. First-time sessions
  should check whether this reads as actor management or excessive waiting.
- **Rules are scoped.** Can travels on the flat service lane, not arbitrary 2D
  navigation. Transport uses fixed rail docks and conservative hazard clearance.
  This is not a universal physics simulation.
- **Presentation remains prototype art.** Existing procedural pixels, simple
  NPC poses and generated sound are coherent enough to read but need broader
  human readability/audio/accessibility evaluation. H has no gamepad binding.
- **Coverage limits:** the automatic press-weight variant and every possible
  timing/position configuration have not received complete fresh-route tests.

The implemented relationship progresses from threat, to predictable tool, to
general force, to state/position resource, to a choice about future options.
The fresh runs actually preserve roof access, change a shared circuit with
force/weight, revisit an old wall, and use different mastery chains. That makes
the progression mechanically playable. Whether first-time people articulate
the intended models remains a human playtest question, not an automated claim.
